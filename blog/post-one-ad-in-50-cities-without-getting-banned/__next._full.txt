1d:T174a,<p>A regional mover serves six metros. A staffing agency has driver openings in fourteen. An affiliate marketer&#39;s offer works anywhere in the country. All three want the same thing from Craigslist — the same ad, live in a lot of cities — and Craigslist is specifically designed to stop that from happening.</p>
<p>The rule is <a href="https://itstillworks.com/can-post-craigslist-4148.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">one post per category per city every 48 hours</a>, and posting similar ads across multiple areas is called out in the terms of use. Do it the obvious way — one account, copy, paste, next city — and you&#39;ll be ghosted within a day and blocked within a week.</p>
<p>And yet businesses are in fifty cities on Craigslist every day. Here&#39;s what the ones that last are actually doing.</p>
<h2>1. The account is regional, not national</h2>
<p>Craigslist ties an account to the pattern of where it posts. An account that posts in Phoenix, then Boston, then Miami, then Seattle inside an hour looks like nothing a real person does. Operations that work keep one verified account per region — sometimes per city — and each account only ever posts where it belongs. Each one needs a phone number Craigslist will accept and an email of its own, and each one gets used lightly.</p>
<h2>2. The connection matches the account</h2>
<p>Craigslist notices the connection every post comes from. One office internet line feeding thirty accounts gets all thirty flagged together. Serious posters pair each regional account with its own connection, in or near the region, and never mix them. If that sounds like a lot of infrastructure for a classified ad, it is. It&#39;s the real cost of multi-city posting, and it&#39;s the part every &quot;post to 100 cities&quot; guide hand-waves.</p>
<h2>3. One per city per day, and never a burst</h2>
<p>The 48-hour rule is per category per city, so one ad per city per day is comfortably inside it. What breaks accounts is bursts: fifty posts in an hour, then nothing for a week. That&#39;s a bot&#39;s signature. Fifty cities means fifty posts spread across a working day, at roughly the same cadence the next day. A steady drip also keeps your ad near the top of every section it&#39;s in, because Craigslist sorts by time.</p>
<h2>4. Same offer, different ad</h2>
<p>Craigslist&#39;s duplicate detection is not naive. Swapping the city name and a synonym won&#39;t fool it. What passes is an ad a person in that city could have written: a different opening paragraph, the neighborhood named naturally in the body, titles that vary in structure, a different lead photo where you have one. Three or four genuinely different versions rotated across cities is enough for most operations. One version pasted fifty times is not.</p>
<h2>5. Look like the section</h2>
<p>Every section has a native style. A gigs post reads like a gig: what the task is, what it pays, how to start. A jobs post has compensation and employment type filled in. A vehicle has a price, a year, mileage, and a title status. Readers flag posts that don&#39;t fit, and Craigslist watches what gets flagged.</p>
<h2>6. Real contact info, every time</h2>
<p>A phone number that answers, an email that replies, or a link that works. Ads with no way to respond, or with obviously fake details, are flagged fast and hurt the account that posted them. If you don&#39;t want calls, Craigslist lets you say so — use the settings instead of leaving fields blank.</p>
<h2>7. Confirm before you count it</h2>
<p>After every post, someone checks the section from a private browser window. If the post isn&#39;t publicly visible after half an hour, it&#39;s ghosted, and the right move is to stop posting from that account for two days — not to post it again. Tracking which posts are actually live is the difference between a multi-city campaign and a multi-city illusion.</p>
<h2>The honest math</h2>
<p>Put the seven together for fifty cities and you get: fifty verified accounts, fifty phone numbers that pass verification, fifty connections, a schedule that spreads fifty posts across every working day, a person checking every post in a private window, and replacement accounts for the ones that die anyway. Call it three to four hours a day of careful work plus a few hundred dollars a month of infrastructure — before a single Craigslist fee.</p>
<p>Most businesses try it once the easy way, lose the accounts, and stop. The ones still posting in fifty cities either built the operation above or pay someone who did.</p>
<h2>The mistakes that end operations</h2>
<ul>
<li>Posting the whole list on day one from a new account.</li>
<li>One account, one connection, many cities.</li>
<li>The same template everywhere, including the same typo.</li>
<li>Stock photos.</li>
<li>Repeatedly re-posting a ghosted ad.</li>
<li>Ignoring the section&#39;s required fields because &quot;nobody reads those.&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>Any one of these costs you an account. Two or three cost you every account you own.</p>

<p>Everything above is our standard operating procedure, because it&#39;s the only version that survives:</p>
<ul>
<li>Separate verified accounts and connections per region. You never touch a Craigslist login.</li>
<li>One post per city per day, paced across the day, up to five posts per city spread across days.</li>
<li>Your ad, reviewed before posting, with the section&#39;s required details filled in.</li>
<li>A public-visibility check on every post before we send you the link. Not visible, not delivered, not charged.</li>
<li>Live links for every post in your inbox and on your order page. Removed within 7 days? Reposted once, free.</li>
</ul>
