Craigslist posting fees in 2026, section by section

What Craigslist actually charges to post — by section, and for gigs and jobs, by city — and why the number you see at checkout is different from the number in every blog post about it.

Craigslist's own posting fees page is short: a list of the sections that cost money and a range for each. It's accurate and it's nearly useless for planning, because two of the ranges — gigs and jobs — span a factor of seven or more depending on the city, and the exact number only appears at the very end of the posting flow.

Here is the full picture as of this writing.

The official schedule

Section What Craigslist charges Where
Jobs $10–75 per post, varies by area US, parts of Canada
Gigs $3–10 per post, varies by area US, selected Canadian areas
Services $5 per post US and Canada
Cars & trucks — by dealer $5 per post US
Cars, trucks, RVs, motorcycles — by owner $5 per post US
For sale — by dealer $5 per post US
Apartment rentals $5 per post New York, Boston, Chicago areas only
Commercial real estate $5 per post US
Everything else Free —

"Everything else" covers most of for sale by owner, most of housing outside the three big rental markets, all of community, and personals-style categories that no longer exist. If you're a private party selling a bicycle, Craigslist is still free. If you're a business posting anything with a commercial angle, it almost certainly isn't.

Gigs: $3 to $10, by metro

Craigslist tiers gigs fees by market size. From our own posting history across 413 US metros, the tiers break roughly like this:

Gigs fee Typical metros
$10 New York, SF Bay Area, Los Angeles, Dallas, Miami
$7 Chicago, Phoenix, Atlanta, Seattle, Washington DC, Houston, Boston, Austin, San Diego, Denver, Tampa, Portland
$5 Minneapolis, Philadelphia, Orlando, Detroit, San Antonio, Pittsburgh, Orange County, Charlotte, Columbus, Baltimore, Nashville, Kansas City, and about 50 similar metros
$3 Everywhere else — roughly 330 smaller markets, from Salt Lake City and Albuquerque down to single-county regions

The exact fee for any city is on its city page and next to each city when you pick them in the pricing calculator.

Jobs: $10 to $75, by metro

Jobs is the expensive one, and the widest spread. Craigslist doesn't publish a per-city list; the figures below come from a published per-city compilation cross-checked against what posters report at checkout, and our team confirms them city by city as we post.

Jobs fee Typical metros
$75 SF Bay Area
$45 New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Boston, Dallas, Denver, Seattle, Phoenix, Minneapolis, South Florida
$35 Atlanta, Houston, Austin, Detroit, Charlotte, Tampa, Kansas City, Columbus, Baltimore, Inland Empire, Hawaii
$25 Most other metro areas: Cincinnati, Cleveland, Indianapolis, Boise, Albuquerque, Fresno, Jacksonville, Buffalo, Hartford
$15–20 Smaller metros and college towns
$10 Rural and micro markets

If you're hiring in ten cities, the same job post can cost anywhere from $100 to $450 in Craigslist fees alone. That's the number that surprises people.

Why every blog post about this is wrong

Three reasons:

  1. Craigslist changes fees without announcing it. The gigs fee didn't exist a few years ago. Services went from free to $5. Rental fees were added to Boston and Chicago after New York. Any article older than a year is suspect, including the one you're reading — which is why we date it.
  2. The per-city numbers aren't published anywhere official. Everyone's list is somebody's compilation.
  3. The fee is shown at the end of the flow. Most people quoting fees have posted in two or three cities and are extrapolating.

Our approach: the fee table that prices every Scale Classifieds order is maintained by the people posting. When a poster sees a different number at Craigslist's checkout than our table says, the table gets corrected and marked verified. It's not perfect on day one; it gets more accurate with every post.
