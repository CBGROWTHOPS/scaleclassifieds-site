1d:T16af,<p><em>The dealer asked us not to use his name or his lot&#39;s. Everything else is as he told it.</em></p>
<p>Ray runs a sixty-unit independent lot on a four-lane road outside a mid-size Southern city. Trucks, mid-size SUVs, a few sedans under $15,000. He&#39;s been selling cars for twenty-two years and has watched every &quot;free leads&quot; channel arrive, get crowded, and get expensive.</p>
<p>Facebook Marketplace was the one that stung. For a couple of years his inventory-management software pushed every vehicle to Marketplace automatically. Then, on September 13, 2021, <a href="https://forum.dealerrefresh.com/threads/rip-facebook-marketplace-for-car-dealers.7492/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Facebook stopped distributing partner inventory feeds to Marketplace</a>, and in January 2023 it <a href="https://www.autosweet.com/blog/facebook-canceling-marketplace-auto-listings-starting-january-2023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">disabled the dealer listing feature entirely</a>. Dealers were left with two options: have employees post cars by hand from personal profiles, or pay for Automotive Inventory Ads.</p>
<p>&quot;Overnight the feed was gone,&quot; Ray says. &quot;My BDC girl was typing in VINs one at a time on her lunch break.&quot;</p>
<h2>Back to Craigslist</h2>
<p>Ray had mostly abandoned Craigslist years earlier, when the site started charging dealers to post. Plenty of dealers did; one industry veteran on the DealerRefresh forums remembers that when Craigslist <a href="https://forum.dealerrefresh.com/threads/rip-facebook-marketplace-for-car-dealers.7492/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">switched to pay-per-post, it "became clutter-free overnight"</a>. The dealers who stayed got a quieter section and buyers who were still very much there.</p>
<p>The fee is small. Craigslist charges <a href="https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/posting_fees" target="_blank" rel="noopener">$5 per cars-and-trucks post by a dealer</a> in the US. Ray&#39;s math: fifteen new arrivals a week, one post each, seventy-five dollars. &quot;I spend more than that on the coffee machine.&quot;</p>
<h2>What he posts</h2>
<p>Ray posts every unit the day it&#39;s front-line ready, and reposts the ones that haven&#39;t moved after their first run expires. Each ad is boring on purpose:</p>
<ul>
<li>Year, make, model, trim, and mileage in the title — nothing else.</li>
<li>Real photos: front three-quarter, rear, dash with the odometer showing, both rows of seats. Twelve to sixteen pictures.</li>
<li>The price. Not &quot;call for price.&quot; Craigslist readers skip ads without one.</li>
<li>Title status, transmission, fuel, drivetrain, and color filled in properly, because buyers filter on them.</li>
<li>Two sentences about the car, the lot&#39;s address, and a phone number marked OK to call and OK to text.</li>
</ul>
<p>&quot;The ads that look like a dealer wrote them get skipped,&quot; he says. &quot;The ads that look like somebody&#39;s uncle wrote them get calls.&quot;</p>

<p>Craigslist leads skew toward cash buyers and trade-ins: people who want a truck this week, not people comparison-shopping across six sites. They also skew local. &quot;Nobody drives two hours off a Craigslist ad. They drive fifteen minutes.&quot;</p>
<p>He hasn&#39;t given up on Marketplace. The manual listings still bring walk-ins, and a couple of dealers on the same forum reported that with a real person answering chats, <a href="https://forum.dealerrefresh.com/threads/rip-facebook-marketplace-for-car-dealers.7492/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Marketplace outperformed their paid listing sites</a> some months. But Marketplace requires an employee to post from a personal account and babysit the inbox. Craigslist requires an ad and five dollars.</p>
<h2>Where it went wrong</h2>
<p>The first month, Ray&#39;s BDC posted the whole lot in one afternoon: sixty ads, one account, one office connection. Within two days most of them vanished from the listings without any notice — the site had quietly hidden them as bulk posting — and the account started demanding phone verification on every attempt.</p>
<p>The fix was pacing and separation: a few posts a day, not sixty; one verified account that only posted for his lot; photos re-shot instead of re-uploaded from the feed; and titles that didn&#39;t all follow the same template. When the volume got beyond what one person could pace by hand, he handed the posting to a service and kept the writing in-house.</p>
<h2>What he&#39;d tell another dealer</h2>
<p>&quot;Don&#39;t post from the dealership Wi-Fi with a fresh account and sixty cars. That&#39;s how you get invisible. Post like a person: a few a day, real pictures, a real number. And put the price on it.&quot;</p>
