4:T1338,<p>You wrote the ad. You paid the fee, if there was one. Craigslist emailed you a confirmation with a link that opens the post just fine. And when you go to the section and look for it — or search for your own title — it isn&#39;t there.</p>
<p>That&#39;s a ghosted post. It&#39;s the single most common Craigslist problem, and the site never tells you it happened.</p>
<h2>First, confirm it&#39;s actually ghosted</h2>
<p>Three quick checks, in order:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Open a private or incognito window</strong> and browse to the section and city you posted in. Sort by newest. Your post should be in the first page or two. Logged-in views of your own account don&#39;t count — you can always see your own ads there.</li>
<li><strong>Search your exact title</strong> from that same incognito window. Ghosted posts don&#39;t appear in search results either.</li>
<li><strong>Wait fifteen minutes and try again.</strong> New posts can take a few minutes to index, and a post with images sometimes lags a little longer. Anything past thirty minutes is a real ghost.</li>
</ol>
<p>If it passes all three, you&#39;re fine. If it fails the first two after half an hour, read on.</p>
<h2>What ghosting is</h2>
<p>Ghosting is Craigslist&#39;s spam filter deciding your post shouldn&#39;t be shown, without deleting it or telling you. The ad looks submitted. Readers can&#39;t find it. From Craigslist&#39;s point of view this is efficient: a spammer who thinks the post went up keeps quietly wasting time instead of adapting.</p>
<p>The catch is that legitimate posts trip the same filter every day.</p>
<h2>The usual triggers</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://smallbusiness.chron.com/prevent-ghost-posting-craigslist-46418.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">advice on the subject</a> has been consistent for years, and it points at four things:</p>
<p><strong>Behavior that looks like bulk posting.</strong> Many posts in a short window from one account. The same ad, or nearly the same ad, in several cities or categories. Several accounts posting similar ads from one connection. Craigslist&#39;s limit is one post per category per city every 48 hours, and it watches for anything that looks like an attempt to get around it.</p>
<p><strong>Content that looks like spam.</strong> Titles in all caps or stuffed with symbols. Broken HTML from a pasted document. The same stock photo it has seen on a thousand other ads. Blocks of keywords at the bottom. Phone numbers written as &quot;three-oh-five&quot; to dodge filters — the filter notices that too.</p>
<p><strong>Account and connection history.</strong> A brand-new account posting a business-style ad. An account that has had posts flagged before. A connection that other, ghosted accounts have used.</p>
<p><strong>The wrong category.</strong> A services ad in gigs, a for-sale ad in community. Readers flag it and the filter learns.</p>
<h2>Why the obvious fixes don&#39;t stick</h2>
<p>The first instinct is to post it again. That&#39;s the worst move: it looks exactly like the behavior that got the post ghosted, and it usually gets the account throttled harder.</p>
<p>The second instinct is to wait a couple of days, rewrite the ad, and try once more. That sometimes works — once. The account and the connection carry their history with them, so the next ad that looks even slightly business-like starts from a worse position than the last one. After a few rounds the account is effectively dead, and the fix people land on is a new account, on a new phone number, on a different connection.</p>
<p>That&#39;s the moment a classified ad turns into a second job. You&#39;re no longer writing ads; you&#39;re managing accounts, numbers, and connections, and checking every post in a private window to see whether it exists. It&#39;s doable. It isn&#39;t worth your time.</p>
<h2>What actually works</h2>
<p>Ghosting is a volume-and-pattern problem, so it&#39;s solved with structure, not clever wording:</p>
<ul>
<li>Separate verified accounts per region, each used lightly and only where it belongs.</li>
<li>One post per category per city per day, paced across the day, never in bursts.</li>
<li>Ads reviewed before they go up: plain text, real photos, the section&#39;s fields filled in, contact details that match.</li>
<li>A public-visibility check on every single post, so a ghost is caught the same hour instead of discovered a week later.</li>
</ul>
<p>That is our standard operating procedure at Scale Classifieds, because it&#39;s the only version that holds up at volume. If a post doesn&#39;t appear, we don&#39;t count it as delivered and you aren&#39;t charged for it. If Craigslist removes a live post within 7 days, we repost it once at no charge.</p>
