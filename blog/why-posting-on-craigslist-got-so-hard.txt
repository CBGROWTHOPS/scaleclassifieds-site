1d:T1a5d,<p>Craigslist still works. That&#39;s the frustrating part. The audience is there — people looking for a job, a gig, an apartment, a truck, a plumber — and the site is one of the few places online where a plain text ad from a small business gets read next to everyone else&#39;s. What changed is how much friction stands between you and a live post.</p>
<p>Here is what people complain about, in roughly the order they run into it.</p>
<h2>1. The free sections aren&#39;t all free anymore</h2>
<p>Craigslist was free for almost everything for almost twenty years. Today its <a href="https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/posting_fees" target="_blank" rel="noopener">posting fees page</a> lists a growing set of paid sections: jobs at <strong>$10–75 depending on the area</strong>, gigs at <strong>$3–10</strong>, services at <strong>$5</strong> everywhere in the US, cars and trucks by dealer at $5, apartment rentals in New York, Boston and Chicago at $5, commercial real estate at $5. Everything else is still free — but &quot;everything else&quot; is mostly private parties selling a couch.</p>
<p>The fee itself isn&#39;t the problem. The problem is that it varies by city and section, it&#39;s only revealed at the end of the posting flow, and there&#39;s no published table. If you post the same job ad in twenty metros you&#39;re paying twenty different prices and you won&#39;t know the total until you&#39;ve done it twenty times.</p>
<h2>2. One post, per category, per city, every 48 hours</h2>
<p>Craigslist&#39;s rule is simple and strict: <a href="https://itstillworks.com/can-post-craigslist-4148.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">you can post in a single category in a given area once every 48 hours</a>. Posting the same ad, or a lightly reworded version of it, in more than one area is a violation of the terms of use, and doing it from one account is the fastest way to get that account throttled.</p>
<p>For a person selling one bike this never matters. For a staffing company with openings in eight cities, a mover who serves three metros, or a dealer with forty units, it is the entire problem. The rule doesn&#39;t scale, and the site is built to notice when you try to make it scale.</p>
<h2>3. Ghosting: the post that isn&#39;t there</h2>
<p>This is the complaint that produces the most support tickets. You post, you pay, you get the confirmation email, you click &quot;manage&quot; and the ad is right there — and it doesn&#39;t appear in the category listing or in search. Nobody sees it. Craigslist doesn&#39;t tell you.</p>
<p>The triggers are well known: too many posts in a short window, similar ads across cities or categories from one connection, several accounts posting similar listings. Ghosting is Craigslist&#39;s spam filter working as designed. It just doesn&#39;t say so. The <a href="https://smallbusiness.chron.com/prevent-ghost-posting-craigslist-46418.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">advice from small-business columnists</a> hasn&#39;t changed in a decade: vary your wording, don&#39;t post in bursts, don&#39;t paste HTML, don&#39;t use the same account for everything. Sound advice that is nearly impossible to follow by hand at any real volume.</p>
<h2>4. Flagged for removal, no reason given</h2>
<p>Any Craigslist reader can flag any post. Enough flags and it&#39;s gone, with an email that says &quot;This posting has been flagged for removal&quot; and nothing else. Competitors flag competitors. Readers flag anything that looks like a business in a section they think should be private-party. Sometimes the software flags on its own.</p>
<p>There is no appeal and no explanation. The only move is to look hard at your own ad — pushy language, prices left off, stock photos, a title in all caps — fix what a suspicious reader would object to, and post again. Which brings you back to rule number 2.</p>
<h2>5. Phone verification, again and again</h2>
<p>New accounts must verify with a phone number that can receive a code, and Craigslist is picky about which numbers it accepts. Worse, verification isn&#39;t one-and-done. Switching accounts on one connection, or using one account from several connections, can trip verification all over again. People who post for a living keep a drawer of phone numbers for exactly this reason.</p>
<h2>6. IP blocks and account bans</h2>
<p>Keep pushing and Craigslist stops talking to you altogether: the site loads a blank page or a block notice, and every account you&#39;ve touched from that connection is suspect. Coming back takes all three of a new connection, a new account with fresh phone verification, and rewritten ad copy. Not one of them. All three.</p>
<h2>7. There is no one to call</h2>
<p>Craigslist has no phone support and no ticketing system for posting problems. The help pages explain the rules; they don&#39;t review your case. Every problem above is yours to diagnose from the outside, usually by searching a forum and guessing.</p>
<h2>What all of this is actually about</h2>
<p>Every item on the list is a symptom of the same thing: Craigslist is built for individuals posting one thing, occasionally, and it treats anyone who looks like a business posting many things, regularly, as a problem to filter. The tools that catch spammers — fees, rate limits, ghosting, phone verification, IP bans — catch legitimate businesses too, because from the outside the behavior looks the same.</p>
<p>You can fight the filter, which means maintaining accounts, numbers, connections and pacing rules yourself. Or you can hand the posting to people who already do.</p>
