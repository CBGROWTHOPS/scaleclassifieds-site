How long a Craigslist ad stays on page one

We measured 25 markets across four categories. A for-sale listing in Los Angeles is off page one in six minutes. An office listing in Detroit holds it for eleven days. Posts expire after 30 days, but that was never the number that mattered.

A Craigslist post expires after 30 days. Almost everybody treats that as the number that matters, and almost nobody checks the one that actually does.

We measured it in October 2026, across 25 of the busiest Craigslist markets and four categories. A for-sale listing in Los Angeles is pushed off page one in about six minutes. An office and commercial listing in Detroit holds page one for eleven days. Same site, same week, a spread of roughly 2,700 to one.

Why page one is the only number that counts

Craigslist shows 120 results per page and sorts them newest first. There is no ranking, no relevance score, nothing to optimise. Every listing posted after yours pushes yours down one slot, and once 120 newer listings exist, you are on page two.

Almost nobody goes to page two.

So a post has two lifespans. The official one is 30 days, after which it expires. The real one is however long it takes the market to produce 120 newer listings — and in a busy category that can be minutes.

What we found

For each market and category we read the live listing feed, took the timestamp of the newest listing and the timestamp of the 120th, and measured the gap. That gap is how long a new post holds page one.

Where a market has fewer than 120 live listings in a category, nothing can displace a post before it expires, so it holds page one for the full month.

Three things worth drawing out

For sale is a different sport. Los Angeles carries 167,806 live for-sale listings and takes roughly 39,000 new ones a day. New York takes 25,000. At that rate page one is a six-minute window, and a single post is close to meaningless — which is why the people who actually sell on Craigslist post continuously rather than once.

Jobs and office listings behave like real markets. Most metros sit between one and three days. That is slow enough that a considered posting schedule works, and fast enough that posting once a month means being invisible for most of it.

The quiet markets are the bargain, and almost nobody exploits them. A market with under 120 live listings in a category gives you page one for the entire 30 days from a single post. The same money that buys six minutes of visibility in Los Angeles buys a month of it somewhere smaller.

What this means for how often to post

There is no universal answer, which is the point. The honest cadence for a given listing is a function of its market and its category, and those two numbers vary by more than three orders of magnitude.

Roughly:

  • Minutes to an hour — for sale in any large metro. Continuous posting or nothing.
  • One to three days — jobs and office listings in most metros. A post every few days holds the front page.
  • A week or more — mid-size and smaller markets. Weekly to monthly is genuinely enough.
  • The full month — any category thin enough to have fewer than 120 live listings. Post once.

The caveats, because they matter

This is a snapshot, and the rate moves with the day of the week. We measured Los Angeles office and commercial twice, 24 hours apart, and got 26 hours and then 47 — weekend posting is slower, and that single variable moved the answer by almost double. Treat any one reading as a band rather than a figure.

Craigslist also publishes no API for this. The numbers come from reading the public listing feed each market serves, which is the same data any visitor sees.

We re-measure every market monthly, and the current figures for each one sit on its city page.
